## Writing should feel native

MeroDoc started from a simple frustration: most writing tools treat Nepali as an afterthought. The product is built around the language, with spelling assistance, a dictionary, and voice input that are part of the writing flow rather than separate utilities.

The editor runs in the browser and is designed to work offline. That constraint shaped the architecture: local storage, service-worker caching, and background processing are part of the product, not optional extras.

## Keeping the interaction light

Speech recognition and dictionary lookups can easily make a text editor feel sluggish. Heavy work is moved into Web Workers, while the interface keeps a clear separation between writing, checking, and exporting.

MeroDoc is a useful example of a small product where the value comes from many small decisions fitting together: language support, offline behavior, privacy, and a UI that does not get in the way.
